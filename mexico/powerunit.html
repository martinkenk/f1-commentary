Mexico City power and energy limits

The FIA power-unit information sheet describes a 4.304 km circuit. Maximum recharge is 8.5 MJ/lap in qualifying, 9.0 MJ in free practice and non-race out-laps, and 8.5 / 9.0 MJ in the race with Overtake inactive / active. The power-limited distance is 2,101 m at a 100 kW/s reduction rate.

For Sprint and Race, the FIA labels the main Overtaking Zones Base-Standard with Overtake inactive and Base-Overtake when active; other lap sections use Alt 1. In practice, including qualifying, the sheet specifies Base-Overtake. The separate Straight Mode markings are active-aero zones, not electrical Overtake lines.

The alternative power curve applies at T4–T10 (2,000–2,900 m) and T12–T16 (3,600–3,950 m). The 350 kW reduction exceptions cover T5–T6 (2,100–2,200 m), T7–T10 (2,550–2,900 m), and T12–T16 / Exit T16 (3,550–3,940 m and 3,950–4,300 m). A reset is permitted at Exit T17 (4,050–4,300 m); the T8–T9 higher-speed threshold is 250 km/h over 2,600–2,800 m. Overtake uses a 1.0 s detection gap, detection at 3,871 m (L20), and activation at 4,051 m (L21 / SC1).

Power-unit elements and separate sanction

The new-elements report lists Valtteri Bottas's fifth ICE and turbocharger, and fourth MGU-K, energy store and control electronics; each exceeds its season allowance in the FIA report. Stewards' Document 17 separately imposed a 50-place drop for the next Race (original ruling). The usage snapshot and penalty are separate records; see the Penalties page for the ruling.

FIA Document 64 lists Liam Lawson's fifth ICE, turbocharger and exhaust against four permitted, and seventh PU-ANC against six. It reports the power unit elements were replaced without approval and the suspension changed under parc fermé; the separate Document 65 requires a pit-lane start (technical report · Stewards' decision).
