Car Development & Upgrades

Hungary is a big upgrade weekend — headlined by Aston Martin's B-spec overhaul, plus a rear-wing arms race up and down the grid.

Storyline to follow

Aston Martin B-spec — the overhaul begins

Aston Martin's much-anticipated B-spec car starts rolling out at the Hungaroring — the first of a two-step upgrade split across Hungary and Zandvoort, with the Dutch GP step arriving alongside Honda's sole 2026 engine upgrade. This is the team's first real attempt to move on from what they've called "the tough times".

What's actually changed on the car

  • New-look sidepod, floor and rear wing were on the cars in the garage by Friday morning (barely visible on Thursday).
  • A bigger 'mouse hole' on the floor and a different rear-wing actuator spotted trackside.
  • Clear modifications to the bargeboard area and sidepod profile.
  • Chassis & gearbox architecture unchanged, but weight taken out of both — forced a re-homologation and crash-test of the forward chassis.
  • Front suspension unchanged; rear suspension slightly revised.
  • New nose and substantially revised aero surfaces — "a big aerodynamic package coupled with significant weight reduction," targeting the weight limit.

The two-step plan & what to watch

  • Step 1 — Hungary: new bodywork/floor/wing debut; back-to-back data vs the old spec.
  • Step 2 — Zandvoort: the bigger step, landing with Honda's 2026 engine upgrade (see Power Unit page).
  • A move "in a different direction" from extreme choices such as the car's aggressive rear ride height.
  • Krack framed parts supply as a "$1 million question" — whether they'd have enough to run the package on both cars — but was "quite confident we will be ready".
  • A big test of the team's data and simulation tools: no winter testing, so they won't extract everything on run one.
When you bring substantial upgrades, it's something the track team will have to learn as quick as possible... We do not expect that we get everything out of it in the first run tomorrow. We need to learn how to manage this.
— Mike Krack, Chief Trackside Officer (The Race, Fri 24 Jul)
We didn't start serious work on the '26 car until mid-March 2025... that left us several months behind our rivals — a huge gap to close.
— Adrian Newey
FP1/FP2 watch: first back-to-back numbers on the upgraded AMR26 vs its old spec — watch the early aero-rake and flow-viz runs, and whether both cars actually run the full package. Basic set-up work (ride height, rakes) will roll on over the next events, so don't expect a step-change immediately.

The rear-wing arms race

Ferrari — 'Macarena' wing

  • FIA-confirmed: new rear-wing flap design with extra trailing-edge devices, re-shaped endplate & new pillars.
  • An optional winglet cascade in the SM-fairing volume — adds rear downforce at the cost of efficiency.
  • Borrows from McLaren's Monaco solution — a triple-element series in the central area.

McLaren — flip-over wing (finally)

  • Revised low-drag 'flip-over' wing fitted to Piastri's car for opening practice.
  • Backed by a genuine 5-item package: new floor & board, rear-wing endplate, revised front & rear corner furniture, bigger brake-cooling exit.
  • Wing test only in Hungary — reverts to the regular wing for qualifying; could debut properly at Monza.

Mercedes — Budapest rear wing

  • FIA-confirmed 3 items: extra rear-wing winglet on the centreline, wider bodywork rear exit (cooling), and the tail winglet merged into the rear-wing brace.
  • All tuned to Budapest's low-speed, high-downforce L/D; paired with a deployment software fix after Russell's Spa woes (see Power Unit page).

Cadillac — brakes upgrade

  • Bolstered brakes package — Bottas said without it they'd 'most likely' not have finished at Spa.
  • Hungaroring is brutally tough on brakes (Cadillac had both cars out by lap 4 at the Red Bull Ring).

Haas — rear wing tweak

  • FIA-confirmed: single item — rear-wing SM-fairing optimisation plus a new, more aggressive Gurney flap for extra rear downforce.
  • Aimed at ending the parts-inconsistency that made the intra-team fight uneven.
