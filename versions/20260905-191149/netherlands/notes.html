The short version for the Dutch Grand Prix — everything worth having to hand.

The circuit in one paragraph

A banked, old-school rollercoaster squeezed into the dunes. Narrow, relentlessly cambered and almost entirely blind — the corners fall away and climb back at you, and the wind off the North Sea changes the balance session to session.

Numbers to have ready

  • Circuit length: 4.259 km
  • Race: 72 laps, 306.587 km
  • First Grand Prix here: 1952 (returned 2021)
  • Lap record: 1:11.097 — Lewis Hamilton, 2021
  • Format: Sprint weekend — one hour of practice only
  • Tyres: C2 / C3 / C4, mandatory race compounds C2 & C3

Talking points

  • A sprint weekend: only one hour of practice before parc fermé, so set-up mistakes are expensive.
  • Wind direction off the sea is the single biggest set-up variable — ask about it in every session.
  • Lawson recalled to Red Bull for the injured Hadjar; Tsunoda back at Racing Bulls.
  • Verstappen re-signed with Red Bull through 2030 days before his home race.
  • Antonelli leads Hamilton by 50 points; Norris won last time out in Hungary.

Name-check these corners

  • Turn 3 — Hugenholtzbocht — Steeply banked (18°) hairpin; cars can run two abreast on different lines, which is what makes the banking more than a gimmick.
  • Turns 7–8 — Scheivlak — Fast, downhill, blind right. Genuinely intimidating and the corner drivers talk about all weekend.
  • Turn 14 — Arie Luyendijkbocht — The final banked (18°) right that launches the cars onto the pit straight — get it wrong and the DRS run to Turn 1 is lost.
