2. Tyre strategy and stint limit2026-11-25

Qatar's 27-lap cap, high pressures and conflicting set totals

Two Formula1.com articles published on 25 November give different per-driver allocations: the 10:05 UTC guide says 2 C1 Hard, 3 C2 Medium and 8 C3 Soft, with an extra Soft reserved for Q3; the 10:43 UTC explainer says each driver gets 13 sets (2 C1, 4 C2, 7 C3). Pirelli's 24 November release confirms the compounds and 27-lap cap, but not set totals. The FIA competition visa confirms 57 race laps over 5.419 km and a 19:00 local start. Its separate Pirelli competition notes prescribe C1 and C2 as mandatory race tyres, C3 for Q3, and 27 laps per set across tyre history; they do not state the conflicting per-driver allocation. The Race's 26 November report says the FIA notes set minimum starting pressures at 29 psi front and 27.5 psi rear, the season's highest front minimum. It attributes front-left overheating, wear and soft graining concerns to Williams, and the risk of an early stop forcing a third stop to McLaren. These are team assessments, not a predicted or published race strategy. Pirelli's reported counting rules include Safety Car and Virtual Safety Car laps; all race laps from start signal to chequered flag count, an unfinished lap in other sessions counts in full, mixed-mileage sets take the higher count, and reconnaissance, formation and post-race laps are excluded.

Why it matters: Keep the conflicting official F1 per-driver allocations explicitly attributed until clarified. The 29/27.5 psi figures are minimum starting pressures; the FIA table separately gives stabilized thresholds of 30/28.5 psi. Attribute the front-left and stop-timing assessments to the named teams, not as measured outcomes or official predicted strategy. Exact remaining-set counts still require a post-qualifying chart.

Sources: Formula1.com, Qatar race-week storylines, checked 25 November / FIA Las Vegas Document 60, visually reviewed / Formula1.com, Vasseur on the Ferrari collision

FIA resolves 2025 financial-regulation cases for Williams and Alpine

The FIA's 25 November 2026 review covered 11 team submissions: the 10 teams that raced in 2025 plus newcomer Cadillac. Nine were compliant with the 2025 financial regulations; Williams had a minor overspend and procedural breach, while Alpine had a procedural breach. All five power-unit manufacturers were compliant. The Williams agreement says the unintentional overspend was £627,000 (0.48%) and found no sporting advantage. Its 12-month sporting sanction subtracts 1.5 percentage points from the coefficient used to set restricted wind-tunnel and CFD allowances (for example, 90% becomes 88.5%); this is not a Qatar grid or race penalty. The Alpine agreement requires a financial penalty and investigation costs, but no sporting penalty. The public summaries do not state either financial-penalty amount.
