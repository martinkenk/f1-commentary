FIA car-display procedure — Document 4

For Friday 27 November, the FIA requires one car from each team to be positioned in its pit-stop position between 15:00 and 16:00 local; the other car is to be available for viewing inside the garage. If only one car carries major aerodynamic or bodywork components not run at a previous Competition or TCC and intended for this event, that is the car to display. The outside car may be used for pit-stop practice, but must return to the same position when practice is not taking place. The notice also sets out an adverse-weather procedure. This governs the media display, not team upgrade declarations, and does not establish that any new components have been submitted. Original FIA Document 4, page 2.

F1 Overtake: detection is at the exit of Turn 16 and activation is 40 m after Turn 16. The map's separate Straight Mode points are not DRS or Overtake points. The separately discovered Lusail Event Document 5 is a Formula 2 map (F2 logo), not the Formula 1 circuit map; its DRS markers do not describe the 2026 F1 system. FIA F1 map · separate FIA support-series map.
FIA Race Director's competition notes, 26 November: the maximum time between the second and first Safety Car Lines for qualifying and race reconnaissance laps will be notified after FP2. During the race, teams receive a lapping pre-warning at 3.0 s; blue panels are shown at 1.2 s, and the lapped driver must yield at the first opportunity. At Turn 16, a track-limits infringement invalidates that lap and the immediately following lap. Race Director's notes, page 2 · track limits, page 6.

Haas confirms Bearman will continue into 2027

On 26 November, TGR Haas confirmed that Oliver Bearman signed a new deal to continue with the team in 2027, completing next year's driver grid. This supersedes the 25 November report that his contract was still being finalised. It confirms his future seat, not a change to the 2026 Qatar entry list; Bearman remains a Haas driver this weekend. TGR Haas official announcement · Formula1.com report.

Pirelli nominated C1 Hard, C2 Medium and C3 Soft for Qatar. Its 13 November graphic marks C3 as the qualifying mandatory set and C1 and C2 as the race mandatory sets. This nomination is not a driver-by-driver remaining-set inventory.

Two Formula1.com articles published on 25 November give different per-driver slick allocations. Its 10:05 UTC tyre guide says two C1 Hard, three C2 Medium and eight C3 Soft sets, and says an extra Soft is reserved for drivers reaching Q3. The 10:43 UTC explainer instead says each driver gets 13 sets: two C1 Hard, four C2 Medium and seven C3 Soft. Pirelli's 24 November release confirms the compounds but does not state these per-driver totals, so the exact allocation is conflicting and remains unresolved here. Formula1.com also says a dry race requires two different slick compounds. These are allocation reports, not post-qualifying remaining-set counts. Formula1.com tyre guide, 10:05 UTC · Formula1.com explainer, 10:43 UTC.

On 24 November, Pirelli announced a maximum 27-lap stint per set, agreed with the FIA, and said the race will require at least two stops. At the full scheduled 57-lap distance, two sets cover only 54 laps. The FIA's Qatar competition notes (Doc 2, page 2) independently specify the same 27-lap limit; the document and its screenshot are linked below. Pirelli's official Qatar tyre briefing, 24 November.

Formula1.com's 25 November explainer quotes Pirelli's counting details: the 27-lap maximum is cumulative across sessions, including Safety Car and Virtual Safety Car laps; all race laps from the start signal to the chequered flag count. In other track sessions, a lap started but not completed counts as a full lap, and a set assembled from tyres with different mileage takes the higher mileage. Reconnaissance, formation and post-race laps are excluded. This report is consistent with the separately reviewed 27-lap FIA/Pirelli Qatar prescription. Formula1.com, 25 November explainer.

The FIA's official Qatar competition notes (Doc 2, page 2) specify slick minimum starting / expected stabilized running pressures of 29.0 / >=30.0 psi at the front and 27.5 / >=28.5 psi at the rear, with camber limits of -2.75 degrees front and -1.50 degrees rear. The same FIA sheet sets C1 and C2 as mandatory race tyres, C3 as the Q3 tyre, and caps each set at 27 laps across all sessions and tyre history. Pirelli's separate 24 November event infographic shows the same pressure figures, marks them subject to change after FP2, and lists an average pit-stop loss of 26.5 seconds; do not present that as a stationary wheel-change duration. These event prescriptions do not resolve the conflicting Formula1.com reports of driver-by-driver set allocations. FIA Competition Notes - Pirelli Preview, page 2 · Pirelli's official Qatar tyre briefing, 24 November.

Pirelli F1 chief engineer Simone Berra's earlier Motorsport.com interview adds details about session-by-session tyre use and remaining allowances after Saturday running. Motorsport.com report and Berra interview, 20 November. Coffee Corner Motorsport's independent 24 November preview says practice and qualifying laps consume each set's allowance. Coffee Corner Motorsport, Qatar tyre preview · Pirelli compound selection and graphic, 13 November.

Two-stop examples — not a prediction

For a full 57-lap dry race, a fresh Medium / fresh Hard / fresh Medium could stop after laps 19 and 38: three 19-lap race stints. An early Safety Car stop after lap 7 could instead be followed by a stop after lap 34: 7 / 27 / 23 laps, if each set has enough allowance left. These are arithmetic examples, not measured optimum windows or a claim that those sets remain available. A shortened race, Safety Car, rain, tyre wear or a changed rule can alter the plan; a dry race also requires two different slick compounds.

Each set's earlier session use reduces its remaining allowance. Formula1.com's 25 November explainer reports that all race laps from the start signal to the chequered flag count; in other sessions, a started but unfinished lap counts in full, and mixed-mileage sets take the higher tyre mileage. Reconnaissance, formation and post-race laps are excluded. These are the reported Pirelli counting rules and match the reviewed FIA/Pirelli event prescription; use post-Saturday official remaining-lap information before applying either example. Formula1.com counting-rule explainer · Coffee Corner Motorsport counting report · Pirelli engineer on remaining laps.

Ferrari's Las Vegas tension carries into Qatar

Formula1.com's 25 November Qatar preview also revisits the Ferrari collision in Las Vegas: Charles Leclerc hit team-mate Lewis Hamilton during their Turn 7 fight. FIA Document 60 found Leclerc wholly responsible and imposed a 10-second penalty; team principal Fred Vasseur described the incident as unacceptable. At Thursday's FIA press conference, Leclerc said the team needed a solution, accepted responsibility for the Vegas clash, and declined to disclose whether the rules of engagement would change. This does not confirm new team rules or a Qatar sanction. Formula1.com reports Hamilton finished seventh after serving a separate 10-second penalty; the final classification is consistent with that result. In a 26 November media-day report, Hamilton said securing second in the Constructors' Championship is Ferrari's immediate priority, that the drivers remain free to race, and that improvements to their processes and communication will be discussed over winter. The FIA press conference also records Leclerc denying a contractual number-one clause; this does not establish operational race instructions. These are updates to ongoing discussions, not a new team rule. Russell and Verstappen move on from their Las Vegas exchange

Formula1.com's 26 November Qatar media-day report says Russell found some of Verstappen's post-race comments "quite amusing", but is moving on and does not want to continue the off-track exchange. Verstappen said there was nothing else to add and that his focus was a good Qatar weekend. These are attributed comments, not a prediction of how they will race each other here. Formula1.com, 26 November.

Red Bull reinforces Hadjar's front wing after Las Vegas failure

In the FIA's Thursday press-conference transcript, Isack Hadjar said the front-wing failure that ended his Las Vegas race remains under investigation. He said the team reinforced the assembly for Qatar and expects it to prevent a repeat. This is the driver's update, not a confirmed engineering root cause. FIA Thursday press-conference transcript, 26 November.

FIA resolves 2025 financial-regulation cases for Williams and Alpine

The FIA's 25 November 2026 review covered 11 team submissions: the 10 teams that raced in 2025 plus newcomer Cadillac. Nine were compliant with the 2025 financial regulations; Williams had a minor overspend and procedural breach, while Alpine had a procedural breach. All five power-unit manufacturers were compliant. The Williams agreement says the unintentional overspend was £627,000 (0.48%) and found no sporting advantage. Its 12-month sporting sanction subtracts 1.5 percentage points from the coefficient used to set restricted wind-tunnel and CFD allowances (for example, 90% becomes 88.5%); this is not a Qatar grid or race penalty. The Alpine agreement requires a financial penalty and investigation costs, but no sporting penalty. The public summaries do not state either financial-penalty amount. FIA's 2025 compliance review · Williams accepted breach agreement · Williams public summary · Alpine accepted breach agreement · Alpine public summary · Formula1.com report.

Haas confirms Rafael Câmara for Qatar and Abu Dhabi

Haas confirmed on 22 November that Câmara will drive for the team at both remaining 2026 Grands Prix, after his race outings in Brazil and Las Vegas. The announcement also identifies him as a full-time Haas driver for 2027. This confirms Câmara's Qatar participation; it does not establish a rookie FP1 substitution or the complete event entry list. Formula1.com, 22 November.

Verified Las Vegas carry-over

Sergio Perez: five-place grid penalty at his next race. FIA Las Vegas Document 61 finds him wholly responsible for contact with Rafael Câmara at Turn 14. Because he retired and could not serve the time penalty, it becomes a grid drop at the next race in which he participates, expected to be Qatar. This is not a five-second Qatar penalty. The circuit in one paragraph

A fast, flowing, medium-to-high-speed loop originally built for motorcycles. Almost no slow corners and very high sustained lateral loads — physically brutal and historically the hardest race of the year on tyre construction.

Name-check these corners

  • Turn 1 — Heavy braking off the main straight; the main passing opportunity. Formula1.com 26 November race-week guide puts the pole-to-braking point run at 403 m.
  • Turns 6–7 — Fast, sustained loading that generates the tyre stress the circuit is known for.
  • Turns 12–14 — Fast, sustained right-hand sequence leading into the final sector.
