2. Tyre strategy and stint limit2026-11-25

Qatar's 27-lap cap, high pressures and conflicting set totals

Two Formula1.com articles published on 25 November give different per-driver allocations: the 10:05 UTC guide says 2 C1 Hard, 3 C2 Medium and 8 C3 Soft, with an extra Soft reserved for Q3; the 10:43 UTC explainer says each driver gets 13 sets (2 C1, 4 C2, 7 C3). Pirelli's 24 November release confirms the compounds and 27-lap cap, but not set totals. The FIA competition visa confirms 57 race laps over 5.419 km and a 19:00 local start. The Race's 26 November report says the FIA notes set minimum starting pressures at 29 psi front and 27.5 psi rear, the season's highest front minimum. It attributes front-left overheating, wear and soft graining concerns to Williams, and the risk of an early stop forcing a third stop to McLaren. These are team assessments, not a predicted or published race strategy. The earlier Pirelli reporting says the cap is cumulative across sessions, including Safety Car and Virtual Safety Car laps. FIA Document 13 V2, published 27 November, clarifies that race laps count when started after the start signal and before the end-of-session signal; reconnaissance and formation laps before the start and laps after the end signal do not. In other sessions, any started lap counts, including practice starts and post-session laps to return to the pits; an unfinished lap counts in full and a mixed set takes the mileage of its highest-mileage tyre. FIA Document 14 V2 page 7 requires a set that exceeds the cap because pit entry was closed to be changed at the earliest safe opportunity after it reopens. If both cars exceed the cap this way, at least one must pit at that opportunity; the second may pass pit entry once before entering.

Why it matters: Keep the conflicting official F1 per-driver allocations explicitly attributed until clarified. The 29/27.5 psi figures are minimum starting pressures; the FIA table separately gives stabilized thresholds of 30/28.5 psi. Attribute the front-left and stop-timing assessments to the named teams, not as measured outcomes or official predicted strategy. Apply the current FIA V2 counting and pit-entry instructions, not the superseded summary of the earlier rule. Exact remaining-set counts still require a post-qualifying chart.

5. Las Vegas fallout2026-11-26

Verstappen/Russell tension and Ferrari clash follow F1 to Qatar

Formula1.com's 25 November Qatar preview recounts the continuing Verstappen-Russell verbal dispute and Ferrari's Las Vegas fallout: Charles Leclerc hit team-mate Lewis Hamilton during their Turn 7 fight. FIA Document 60 found Leclerc wholly responsible and imposed a 10-second penalty; team principal Fred Vasseur described the incident as unacceptable. At Thursday's FIA press conference, Leclerc said Ferrari needed a solution, accepted responsibility for the Vegas clash, and declined to disclose whether the rules of engagement would change. He also denied having a contractual number-one clause. Neither statement establishes the team's operational race instructions. A 26 November media-day report quotes Hamilton saying securing second in the Constructors' Championship is Ferrari's immediate priority, the drivers remain free to race, and process/communication improvements will be discussed over winter. The Race's Qatar day-one report says Antonelli described a Mercedes software safety feature added after the Vegas battery-deployment loss; this is a reported team change, not a change to FIA limits or a validated Qatar outcome. Formula1.com's Russell/Verstappen update adds that Russell found some post-race comments "quite amusing", is moving on and does not want to continue the off-track exchange; Verstappen said there was nothing else to add and that his focus was Qatar.

Why it matters: The Vegas penalty is final and does not become a Qatar ruling. Leclerc's contract denial and the drivers' comments do not establish team race instructions or a new rule. Attribute the Mercedes software update to The Race; do not treat it as an FIA energy-limit change or a confirmed on-track result. Keep Russell and Verstappen quotes attributed, not as predictions.

What the circuit demands

A fast, flowing, medium-to-high-speed loop originally built for motorcycles. Almost no slow corners and very high sustained lateral loads — physically brutal and historically the hardest race of the year on tyre construction.

Weekend format
