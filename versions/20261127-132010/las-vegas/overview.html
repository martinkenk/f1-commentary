2. Ferrari collision2026-11-26

Leclerc and Hamilton address the Las Vegas fallout

After a Turn 7 collision, Leclerc accepted blame; team principal Fred Vasseur called it 'unacceptable' and 'a lack of respect', two races after another clash in Mexico. Formula1.com's reports differ on whether it was Lap 37 or Lap 38, so no lap number is given. Formula1.com confirms Hamilton finished seventh after serving his separate 10-second penalty; the final classification is consistent with the served sanction. At Qatar media day on 26 November, Hamilton said Ferrari's priority is securing second in the Constructors' Championship, the drivers remain free to race, and process/communication improvements will be discussed over winter. This reports ongoing conversations, not a new team rule.

Why it matters: Keep Leclerc's 10-second final-result penalty separate from Hamilton's 10-second penalty served during the race. Hamilton's comments describe Ferrari's current target and future discussions; they announce no new team rules and do not predict the next race.

Sources: Formula1.com, Vasseur on the clash / Formula1.com, Leclerc accepts blame / Formula1.com, Hamilton on his separate penalty / FIA Document 60, visually checked; Turn 7 collision ruling / FIA Document 54, Hamilton ruling / FIA Document 65, final classification / Formula1.com, Leclerc and Hamilton's Qatar media-day comments, 26 November

3. Qatar grid carry-over2026-11-22

Perez has a five-place drop at his next race

The stewards found Perez wholly responsible for contact with Câmara at Turn 14. Because Perez retired, the unserved time penalty was converted to a five-place drop at his next race.

Why it matters: Qatar is the expected next application. This is a grid penalty, not a five-second race penalty, and the Vegas PU sanctions must not be carried over a second time.

Sources: FIA Document 61, visually reviewed / Formula1.com report and driver reactions

4. Hadjar's race2026-11-22

Hadjar's front wing fails; a separate Safety Car penalty demotes him

Hadjar reached the lead battle before his front wing collapsed; he said he did not know the cause. A separate 10-second penalty for driving unnecessarily slowly behind the Safety Car dropped him to 15th.

Why it matters: Keep the wing-failure cause unconfirmed and distinguish it from the Safety Car infringement.

Sources: Formula1.com report, 22 November / FIA Document 64, Safety Car infringement

5. Mercedes late-race energy2026-11-26

Mercedes adds a reported software safeguard after Antonelli's battery loss

Mercedes team principal Toto Wolff said Antonelli lifted behind Verstappen before reapplying, leaving him with no battery on the back straight; both McLarens passed and Antonelli finished fourth. Wolff said Mercedes had planned an energy deployment for the final 400 metres that could have offered a chance to win. The Race's 26 November Qatar report says Antonelli described a safety feature Mercedes added to make the energy-management system more robust after that loss.

Why it matters: Attribute the race account to Wolff and the software change to The Race's report of Antonelli's comments, not independent telemetry. The team-side change is not an FIA energy-limit revision, and its effect at Qatar is not yet demonstrated.

Sources: Formula1.com, Wolff's post-race account / The Race, Qatar day-one report quoting Antonelli, 26 November

What the circuit demands

A cold, late-night, very low-downforce blast down the Strip. Enormous top speeds and almost no corners of consequence — the whole weekend is defined by the struggle to get tyres into their working range on a cold street surface.

Weekend format

Conventional weekend — three practice sessions, Qualifying and the Grand Prix.

  • All session times are shown in Las Vegas (PST) and Tallinn (EET).
  • Live weather is attached to each session on the Schedule & Weather page.
  • Session results appear on the Results page as soon as each session finishes.

Weekend takeaways

  • Track temperature falling through the session changes the tyre picture in real time.
  • Low-downforce trim exaggerates power-unit and deployment differences.

Overtaking picture

Good, thanks to a very long full-throttle stretch into a slow corner. Slipstream effects are large because everyone runs skinny wings.
