1. Practice2026-11-07

Antonelli leads close FP2: the top five are within 0.321s

The official 21-car FP2 classification puts Kimi Antonelli fastest at 1:10.763, 0.253s ahead of Lando Norris; Max Verstappen (+0.259), George Russell (+0.273) and Oscar Piastri (+0.321) complete the top five. Nico Hülkenberg did not set a time. Formula1.com's Saturday Practice Debrief notes the fine margins among the leading teams and asks whether Mercedes can sustain its Friday pace. Stewards' Document 23 lists ten deleted-lap entries for nine drivers (Albon appears twice) after track-use issues at Turns 1 and 4; Race Director's Document 24 sets a below-1:13.0 interval between the Safety Car lines for qualifying and specified reconnaissance laps.

Why it matters: Antonelli leads the Friday sheet, but the top-five spread is only 0.321s and a single-lap classification does not establish race pace. FP3 is the next check on whether Mercedes can stay ahead. The lap-time ruling is not itself a revised classification or a race penalty. The 1:13.0 limit applies only during/after qualifying and on reconnaissance laps while pit exit is open, not to racing laps.

Sources: Formula1.com: Practice Debrief (public excerpt) / Formula1.com: FP2 report / FIA: FP2 classification, Document 22 / Formula1.com: official Practice 2 classification / FIA: FP2 deleted lap times, Document 23 / FIA: SC2–SC1 maximum, Document 24

2. Stewards and grid2026-11-06

Russell gets five-place drop; Camara reprimanded after FP2

FIA Document 14 records Russell's sixth ICE against four allowed; the Stewards' separate Document 15 imposes a five-place grid drop. In Document 25, the Stewards found Rafael Camara unnecessarily impeded Charles Leclerc in FP2 and issued Camara his first driving reprimand of the season.

Why it matters: The rulings have different consequences: Russell's sanction affects his Brazil grid position; Camara's reprimand is not a grid penalty. Neither changes the FP2 classification.

Sources: FIA: Russell's new PU elements, Document 14 / FIA: Russell's grid penalty, Stewards' Document 15 / FIA: Camara's reprimand, Stewards' Document 25 / Formula1.com: Russell's Brazil grid penalty

3. Championship2026-11-04

Antonelli's drivers' title is settled; Mercedes still chasing constructors' crown

Kimi Antonelli clinched the Drivers' Championship in Mexico. The latest official standings show 380 points for Antonelli and 261 for Verstappen; Mercedes leads Ferrari 630-504. Formula1.com's pre-weekend scenario says Mercedes seals the constructors' title if it gains three points on Ferrari in Brazil.

Why it matters: Brazil's practice has not changed the standings, and the constructors' title remains undecided. These are current pre-race totals, not a result projection.

Sources: Formula1.com: official driver standings / Formula1.com: official constructor standings / Formula1.com: constructors' title scenario

4. Technical and circuit2026-11-06

Norris reports an early gain from McLaren's Brazil upgrades

FIA Document 12 lists four McLaren performance items: front wing, rear wing, beam wing and floor furniture. After Friday practice, Norris said the upgrades seemed to gain performance; he topped FP1 and finished second in FP2. The Race reports that the new front-wing concept is intended to carry into McLaren's 2027 launch specification.

Why it matters: The Friday evidence is encouraging but does not establish a race-long gain. Norris's remarks are a driver assessment; check whether the package remains effective through FP3 and qualifying.

Sources: FIA: all-team car presentation submissions, Document 12 / Formula1.com: Norris on McLaren's Friday upgrades / The Race: McLaren's 2027 front-wing concept

Confirmed driver changes

Six rookie stand-ins in FP1

Formula1.com's lineup and the official classification show six drivers took the regular seats for Friday's one-hour FP1:

  • McLaren: Leonardo Fornaroli for Oscar Piastri
  • Ferrari: Dino Beganovic for Charles Leclerc
  • Williams: Luke Browning for Carlos Sainz
  • Audi: Freddie Slater for Nico Hulkenberg
  • Alpine: Gabriele Mini for Pierre Gasly
  • Cadillac: Colton Herta for Sergio Perez

The Race reports Herta picked up his final Super Licence point after FP1, passing the 100 km running requirement when combined with his four other qualifying FP1 outings and IndyCar results. Formula1.com explains the requirement. This clears the point requirement but does not confirm a 2027 seat. Formula1.com lineup · official FP1 classification · Formula1.com FP1 report · The Race on Herta's milestone · FIA Document 13 entry list.

Haas: Rafael Camara's race debut

Rafael Camara partners Oliver Bearman for the whole Sao Paulo weekend, not merely FP1. Haas says Esteban Ocon will not return to its race seat in 2026. Mexico stand-in Ryo Hirakawa has World Endurance Championship commitments this weekend. Camara's early promotion is confirmed for Brazil only; do not assume he will contest the remaining 2026 races. Haas announcement via Formula1.com, 3 November.

Team and grid storylines

Mercedes: constructors' title still in play

Antonelli clinched the Drivers' Championship in Mexico. The latest official standings still show Mercedes leading Ferrari 630–504 (126 points). Formula1.com's pre-weekend scenario says gaining three points on Ferrari in Brazil clinches the constructors' title; practice awards no points, and the title is not yet settled. Official title scenario.

Mercedes: Russell receives a five-place drop

FIA Document 14 reports a new ICE for Russell at the twentieth competition: it is his sixth against four allowed and the Technical Delegate says it is not compliant with Sporting Regulation B8.2.2(a). The Stewards' separate Document 15 now imposes a five-place drop for the next race in which Russell participates. The season-to-date usage report (Document 8) is not an event-specific parts declaration or the penalty decision. FIA Document 14 · FIA Document 15 · Formula1.com report · The Race report. The FIA filings do not establish whether the planned ADUO specification is arriving.

FP1/FP2: FIA lap-time deletions

Stewards' Document 19 deletes the shown FP1 lap times for Franco Colapinto (2:05.129), Alexander Albon (1:32.736), Luke Browning (1:33.848), Rafael Câmara (1:18.760) and Dino Beganovic (OUT LAP) after their cars passed through a double-waved yellow sector. This is a lap-time deletion, not a race penalty or a revised FP1 classification. FIA Document 19 (issued 6 November at 14:00 local).

Document 23 lists ten FP2 lap entries for nine drivers (Albon appears twice) after cars did not use the track at Turns 1 and 4. The listed times are deleted; the decision is not itself a revised FP2 classification or a race penalty. FIA Document 23 · official FP2 classification.

Stewards: Camara receives his first reprimand

FIA Document 25 resolves the Turn 15 summons involving Rafael Camara (Car 51) and Charles Leclerc (Car 16). The Stewards found that Camara unnecessarily impeded Leclerc in FP2 at 16:31; after warnings that Car 16 was on a fast lap, Car 51 moved into the racing line and Leclerc had to take evasive action. The decision is a driving reprimand, Camara's first of the season. It is not a grid drop or a revised FP2 classification. FIA Document 25 · FIA event documents.

Ferrari: Hamilton penalty expected after Friday component changes

The Race reports Ferrari planned power-unit component changes after FP2 and that Hamilton is set to take a Brazil grid penalty. The 13:30 UTC FIA refresh found no Hamilton-specific parts report or Stewards' ruling among the 27 discovered event documents, so this remains an attributed expectation; the exact component and sanction are not confirmed in the FIA documents reviewed. The Race's report · FIA event listing.

McLaren: four declared changes, early gain reported by Norris

FIA Document 12 lists four performance-motivated items: front wing, rear wing, beam wing and floor furniture. The team's stated aims concern flow conditioning and local aerodynamic load. After Friday practice, Norris said the package seemed to gain performance; he topped FP1 and finished second in FP2. This is an early driver assessment, not an independently measured race-long gain. The Race reports that the front-wing concept is intended to carry into McLaren's 2027 launch specification. FIA declaration · Formula1.com: Norris on the upgrades · The Race technical report.

Race control: FIA Race Director's Document 24 sets the maximum interval below 1:13.0 between the Safety Car lines during and after Qualifying, and on reconnaissance laps while the pit exit is open for the Race. This is not a racing-lap maximum. FIA Document 24.

What the circuit demands

Short, anti-clockwise, hilly and permanently interesting. Barely 4.3 km, so the field is compressed and traffic is constant. Weather is famously volatile — sun and a downpour within the same hour is normal.

Weekend format

Conventional weekend — three practice sessions, Qualifying and the Grand Prix.

  • All session times are shown in São Paulo (BRT) and Tallinn (EET).
  • Live weather is attached to each session on the Schedule & Weather page.
  • Session results appear on the Results page as soon as each session finishes.

Storylines to prepare

  • Rain probability is high enough that a wet-weather plan is essential preparation.
  • Late-season championship maths often becomes live here.

Overtaking picture

Very good. A long uphill straight into a downhill braking zone, plus several secondary opportunities. Interlagos reliably produces genuine racing.
