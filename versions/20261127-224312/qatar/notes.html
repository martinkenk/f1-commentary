Qatar FIA filing: Audi declares one front-corner update

FIA Document 12 lists one declared item across all eleven teams: Audi's revised lower front brake-duct deflector, filed for performance / flow conditioning. The description says it improves flow-field quality delivered to the rest of the car; this is the team's stated rationale, not an independently measured gain. Ten teams explicitly submitted no updates for Qatar. Audi's component-location diagram is on page 11. Audi declaration, page 10 · component diagram, page 11 · Full team-by-team filing and readers.

FIA car-display procedure — Document 4

For Friday 27 November, the FIA requires one car from each team to be positioned in its pit-stop position between 15:00 and 16:00 local; the other car is to be available for viewing inside the garage. If only one car carries major aerodynamic or bodywork components not run at a previous Competition or TCC and intended for this event, that is the car to display. The outside car may be used for pit-stop practice, but must return to the same position when practice is not taking place. The notice also sets out an adverse-weather procedure. This governs the media display, not team upgrade declarations, and is distinct from the car-presentation submissions recorded in FIA Document 12. Original FIA Document 4, page 2.

Friday's FIA rulings: Document 17 lists 32 deleted-lap entries for 12 drivers at Turns 1–6, 8, 10 and 14–16 in FP1; its table and ruling are on pages 2–3. Document 18 finds an unsafe pit-box release involving Car 6 / Isack Hadjar and fines Oracle Red Bull Racing €30,000, with €20,000 suspended for 12 months. A team member's foot touched the rear-left wheel as the car began to leave with a brake-cooling blower still fitted. Document 19 confirms Sergio Perez was 22 minutes late to the Thursday press conference but applies no penalty, after an insurance issue delayed his flight and road closures delayed arrival. These are separate decisions, not a new grid penalty. Document 20 fines Audi €500 after Nico Hülkenberg's Car 27 was measured at 84.4 km/h in the 80 km/h pit lane during FP2; the fine is against the competitor, not the driver. FIA curfew report — Document 10

Issued to the stewards on 27 November, the Technical Delegate's report says BWT Alpine Formula One Team personnel associated with car operation were inside the circuit during a 13-hour curfew period from 22:30 on Wednesday 25 November to 11:30 on Thursday 26 November. The period was 42 to 29 hours before the scheduled start of P1. This was Alpine's second of six permitted individual exceptions for the 2026 season, so the report says no action should be taken; it records no driver penalty. Original FIA Document 10.

Document 13 V2 clarifies the 27-lap cap's cumulative count across all track sessions, including laps under the Safety Car or Virtual Safety Car. For the Race, count each lap started after the start signal and before the end-of-session signal; reconnaissance and formation laps before the start and laps after the end signal do not count. In every other session, any lap started counts, including post-session laps to return to the pits and practice-start laps. A lap started but not completed counts in full, and a mixed set takes the mileage of its highest-mileage tyre. Document 14 V2 repeats this rule and clarifies that a set exceeding its limit because pit entry was closed must be changed at the earliest safe opportunity after it reopens; when both cars of a team exceed the limit this way, at least one must pit at that opportunity. The second car may pass pit entry once before entering. Formula1.com's 25 November explainer describes the earlier rule. Formula1.com, 25 November explainer · FIA Document 13 V2, page 2 · FIA Document 14 V2, page 7.

Two-stop examples — not a prediction

For a full 57-lap dry race, a fresh Medium / fresh Hard / fresh Medium could stop after laps 19 and 38: three 19-lap race stints. An early Safety Car stop after lap 7 could instead be followed by a stop after lap 34: 7 / 27 / 23 laps, if each set has enough allowance left. These are arithmetic examples, not measured optimum windows or a claim that those sets remain available. A shortened race, Safety Car, rain, tyre wear or a changed rule can alter the plan; a dry race also requires two different slick compounds.

Each set's earlier session use reduces its remaining allowance. FIA Documents 13 and 14 V2 clarify that post-session laps to return to the pits and practice-start laps count against the cap, while reconnaissance and formation laps before the race start do not. If pit entry closure causes a set to exceed the cap, it must be changed at the earliest safe opportunity after pit entry reopens. Use the official post-Saturday remaining-lap information before applying either example. Ferrari's Las Vegas tension carries into Qatar

Formula1.com's 25 November Qatar preview also revisits the Ferrari collision in Las Vegas: Charles Leclerc hit team-mate Lewis Hamilton during their Turn 7 fight. FIA Document 60 found Leclerc wholly responsible and imposed a 10-second penalty; team principal Fred Vasseur described the incident as unacceptable. At Thursday's FIA press conference, Leclerc said the team needed a solution, accepted responsibility for the Vegas clash, and declined to disclose whether the rules of engagement would change. This does not confirm new team rules or a Qatar sanction. Formula1.com reports Hamilton finished seventh after serving a separate 10-second penalty; the final classification is consistent with that result. In a 26 November media-day report, Hamilton said securing second in the Constructors' Championship is Ferrari's immediate priority, that the drivers remain free to race, and that improvements to their processes and communication will be discussed over winter. The FIA press conference also records Leclerc denying a contractual number-one clause; this does not establish operational race instructions. These are updates to ongoing discussions, not a new team rule. FIA resolves 2025 financial-regulation cases for Williams and Alpine

The FIA's 25 November 2026 review covered 11 team submissions: the 10 teams that raced in 2025 plus newcomer Cadillac. Nine were compliant with the 2025 financial regulations; Williams had a minor overspend and procedural breach, while Alpine had a procedural breach. All five power-unit manufacturers were compliant. The Williams agreement says the unintentional overspend was £627,000 (0.48%) and found no sporting advantage. Its 12-month sporting sanction subtracts 1.5 percentage points from the coefficient used to set restricted wind-tunnel and CFD allowances (for example, 90% becomes 88.5%); this is not a Qatar grid or race penalty. The Alpine agreement requires a financial penalty and investigation costs, but no sporting penalty. The public summaries do not state either financial-penalty amount. The circuit in one paragraph

A fast, flowing, medium-to-high-speed loop originally built for motorcycles. Almost no slow corners and very high sustained lateral loads — physically brutal and historically the hardest race of the year on tyre construction.

Name-check these corners

  • Turn 1 — Heavy braking off the main straight; the main passing opportunity. Formula1.com 26 November race-week guide puts the pole-to-braking point run at 403 m.
  • Turns 6–7 — Fast, sustained loading that generates the tyre stress the circuit is known for.
  • Turns 12–14 — Fast, sustained right-hand sequence leading into the final sector.
