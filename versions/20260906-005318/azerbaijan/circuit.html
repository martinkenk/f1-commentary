Baku City Circuit Circuit Guide

A contradiction: a 2.2 km flat-out blast along the seafront bolted onto a medieval old-town section barely wide enough for one car. Teams must choose between straight-line speed and the confidence to run close to the walls.

Corners that matter

Turn 1

Tight left after the start; a regular first-lap incident spot.

Turns 8–12 — the castle section

The narrowest point on the calendar (~7.6 m) — walls on both sides and no room to correct.

Turn 16 into the main straight

Exit here defines the whole 2.2 km run to the line; the single most important corner of the lap.

Where the passes happen

Excellent. The enormous DRS-assisted straight into a tight Turn 1 braking zone creates late lunges and slipstream chess. Low-drag cars can pass; high-downforce cars have to defend.

What it does to the tyres

Low abrasion but severe warm-up difficulty — getting the fronts switched on for a single lap is the classic Baku headache, especially in qualifying.

Worth knowing

Circuit notes

  • Baku has produced some of the most chaotic races of the modern era; safety cars are close to a statistical certainty.
  • First Grand Prix: 2016 (as the European Grand Prix), 2017 onwards as the Azerbaijan Grand Prix.
  • Wind off the Caspian regularly swings the balance between sectors.
