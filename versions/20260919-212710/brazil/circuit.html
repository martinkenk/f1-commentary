Autódromo José Carlos Pace, Interlagos Circuit Guide

Short, anti-clockwise, hilly and permanently interesting. Barely 4.3 km, so the field is compressed and traffic is constant. Weather is famously volatile — sun and a downpour within the same hour is normal.

Corners that matter

Turns 1–2 — Senna S

Downhill left-hander at the end of the main straight; the classic overtaking spot and a lap-one flashpoint.

Turn 4 — Descida do Lago

Downhill left where cars can run side by side.

Subida dos Boxes

The long, climbing final sequence onto the pit straight — exit speed here sets up the next lap's pass.

Where the passes happen

Very good. A long uphill straight with DRS into a downhill braking zone, plus several secondary opportunities. Interlagos reliably produces genuine racing.

What it does to the tyres

Anti-clockwise loading stresses the right-hand tyres and the neck. The surface is bumpy and the short lap means high stint lap counts and traffic management.

Worth knowing

Circuit notes

  • First Grand Prix: 1973.
  • Anti-clockwise, one of only a handful on the calendar.
  • The circuit has decided multiple World Championships and the crowd knows it.
