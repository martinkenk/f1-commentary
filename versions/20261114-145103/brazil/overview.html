Confirmed driver changes

Six rookie stand-ins in FP1

Formula1.com's lineup and the official classification show six drivers took the regular seats for Friday's one-hour FP1:

  • McLaren: Leonardo Fornaroli for Oscar Piastri
  • Ferrari: Dino Beganovic for Charles Leclerc
  • Williams: Luke Browning for Carlos Sainz
  • Audi: Freddie Slater for Nico Hulkenberg
  • Alpine: Gabriele Mini for Pierre Gasly
  • Cadillac: Colton Herta for Sergio Perez

The Race reports Herta picked up his final Super Licence point after FP1, passing the 100 km running requirement when combined with his four other qualifying FP1 outings and IndyCar results. Formula1.com explains the requirement. This clears the point requirement but does not confirm a 2027 seat. Formula1.com lineup · official FP1 classification · Formula1.com FP1 report · The Race on Herta's milestone · FIA Document 13 entry list.

Haas: Rafael Camara's race debut

Rafael Camara partners Oliver Bearman for the whole Sao Paulo weekend, not merely FP1. Haas says Esteban Ocon will not return to its race seat in 2026. Mexico stand-in Ryo Hirakawa has World Endurance Championship commitments this weekend. Camara's early promotion is confirmed for Brazil only; do not assume he will contest the remaining 2026 races. Haas announcement via Formula1.com, 3 November.

Team and grid storylines

Mercedes clinches constructors' title in Sao Paulo

Formula1.com reports Mercedes secured the 2026 Constructors' Championship in Brazil. Antonelli had already clinched the Drivers' title in Mexico. The linked standings page carries the current official championship totals; the pre-race three-point scenario is now settled. Formula1.com race report · official Constructors' standings.

Power-unit penalties shaped Brazil's starting order

FIA Document 30 reports new elements for Verstappen, both Ferraris, Perez and Russell. It records Verstappen's fifth ICE, turbocharger and exhaust against four allowed, and a sixth PU-ANC within the six-part allowance; both Ferrari drivers receive a fifth ICE against four allowed. Perez's report lists a fifth ICE and turbo, fourth MGU-K and fifth PU-ANC. Russell's new PU-ANC is sixth of six. These are Technical Delegate counts, not all separate penalty rulings. The season-to-date usage report (Document 8) is a separate earlier snapshot, not an event-specific parts declaration. The FIA filings do not establish whether the planned ADUO specification is arriving. Formula1.com reported 10-place drops for Hamilton and Leclerc; the Stewards' later Document 33 imposed a 30-place drop on Verstappen for the fifth ICE, turbocharger and exhaust. Verstappen qualified 16th and the official Starting Grid placed him 21st. A separate Document 39 imposes a 30-place drop on Perez for his fifth ICE, fifth turbocharger and fourth MGU-K; Perez qualified 21st and the official Starting Grid placed him 22nd. Document 30's fifth PU-ANC for Perez is within that element's allowance. Stewards' Documents 35 and 37 impose 10-place drops for Leclerc (Car 16) and Hamilton (Car 44), respectively, for their fifth ICEs. FIA Document 42 then lists Hamilton's sixth ICE, fifth turbocharger and exhaust, fourth energy store, fourth control electronics unit and MGU-K, and seventh PU-ANC. Document 43 imposes a further 65-place drop for those changes, in addition to Document 37's 10 places, a cumulative 75-place penalty. Car 44 qualified 11th and the official Starting Grid placed Hamilton 20th. The Race's provisional order lists Hamilton 20th; the official Formula1.com Starting Grid now also lists him 20th. Use that published classification rather than the earlier provisional order. This later FIA filing supersedes the earlier team statement that Hamilton would start from the back without specifying component counts. Russell's separate five-place drop followed his sixth ICE (sixth against four allowed) in Document 15. The Technical Delegate's later Document 55 reports Lindblad's seventh ICE and sixth turbocharger (four allowed each), plus Bortoleto's sixth energy store and sixth PU-CE (three allowed each); it states all four are not compliant. This is a component report, not a Stewards' penalty decision. Separate Stewards' Documents 56 and 57 impose 10-place drops for Lindblad (seventh ICE, sixth TC) and Bortoleto (sixth ES, sixth PU-CE). FIA practice lap-time deletions

Stewards' Document 19 deletes the shown FP1 lap times for Franco Colapinto (2:05.129), Alexander Albon (1:32.736), Luke Browning (1:33.848), Rafael Câmara (1:18.760) and Dino Beganovic (OUT LAP) after their cars passed through a double-waved yellow sector. This is a lap-time deletion, not a race penalty or a revised FP1 classification. FIA Document 19 (issued 6 November at 14:00 local).

Document 23 lists ten FP2 lap entries for nine drivers (Albon appears twice) after cars did not use the track at Turns 1 and 4. The listed times are deleted; the decision is not itself a revised FP2 classification or a race penalty. FIA Document 23 · official FP2 classification.

FIA Document 36 also deletes two listed FP3 lap times after the cars did not use the track at Turns 1 and 4: Rafael Camara (Car 51), 1:34.799 at 11:35:37, and Max Verstappen (Car 3), 1:52.648 at 11:57:43. This ruling deletes those entries; it is not itself a revised FP3 classification. FIA Document 36.

FIA Document 44 lists eight deleted Qualifying lap-time entries affecting Rafael Câmara, Gabriel Bortoleto, Lance Stroll, Carlos Sainz, Oliver Bearman and Sergio Perez. Document 46 separately deletes Stroll's 1:42.680 lap for a double-waved-yellow sector. These are lap-time decisions, not additional grid penalties or replacements for the official qualifying classification. FIA Document 44 · FIA Document 46.

Stewards: Camara receives his first reprimand

FIA Document 25 resolves the Turn 15 summons involving Rafael Camara (Car 51) and Charles Leclerc (Car 16). The Stewards found that Camara unnecessarily impeded Leclerc in FP2 at 16:31; after warnings that Car 16 was on a fast lap, Car 51 moved into the racing line and Leclerc had to take evasive action. The decision is a driving reprimand, Camara's first of the season. It is not a grid drop or a revised FP2 classification. FIA Document 25 · FIA event documents.

Cadillac warned after Perez throws loose cockpit item

FIA Document 40 resolves the unsafe-condition summons for Sergio Perez (Car 11). After reviewing in-car video, the Stewards found he threw a small plastic cable organiser that had come loose from the seat belt out of the car during FP3. Cadillac received a formal warning: the Stewards said throwing material onto the track during a live session was inappropriate, even though the item was small and unlikely to damage another car or injure a driver. This is not a grid penalty. FIA Document 40 · resolved summons, Document 31.

FIA issues a severe-weather garage notice

Race Director Document 38 says that, due to anticipated severe weather, all teams are allowed to close their garage doors. It is an operational precaution, not an announcement of a session delay. FIA Document 38 · FIA event documents.

McLaren: four declared changes, early gain reported by Norris

FIA Document 12 lists four performance-motivated items: front wing, rear wing, beam wing and floor furniture. The team's stated aims concern flow conditioning and local aerodynamic load. After Friday practice, Norris said the package seemed to gain performance; he topped FP1 and finished second in FP2. This is an early driver assessment, not an independently measured race-long gain. The Race reports that the front-wing concept is intended to carry into McLaren's 2027 launch specification. FIA declaration · Formula1.com: Norris on the upgrades · The Race technical report.

Race control: FIA Race Director's Document 24 sets the maximum interval below 1:13.0 between the Safety Car lines during and after Qualifying, and on reconnaissance laps while the pit exit is open for the Race. This is not a racing-lap maximum. FIA Document 24.

What the circuit demands

Short, anti-clockwise, hilly and permanently interesting. Barely 4.3 km, so the field is compressed and traffic is constant. Weather is famously volatile — sun and a downpour within the same hour is normal.

Weekend format

Conventional weekend — three practice sessions, Qualifying and the Grand Prix.

Overtaking picture

Very good. A long uphill straight into a downhill braking zone, plus several secondary opportunities. Interlagos reliably produces genuine racing.

Race strategy recap

Observed wet-dry-wet strategy

Formula1.com's report describes a wet-dry-wet 71-lap race: Hamilton was first to switch to Softs at the end of Lap 14; Norris, Russell and Hadjar stopped at the end of Lap 16, followed by Antonelli and Verstappen on Lap 17. Norris made another stop on Lap 49. Rain returned at Lap 61, when Russell and Alonso first changed to Intermediates and the field followed. The pre-race dry strategy projections are retained on Tyres & Strategy as historical estimates, not the race's actual route. FIA Document 81 confirms the final classification after 71 laps and 305.879 km, in agreement with Formula1.com.
