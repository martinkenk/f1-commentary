Complete FIA event document listing

In 2026, active-aero Straight Mode and electrical-power Overtake are separate systems. The FIA publishes a power and energy map for every individual event. Sao Paulo's published map is transcribed below.

What changed for 2026

  • Roughly a 50/50 split between internal combustion and electrical power.
  • The MGU-H is gone; the MGU-K is substantially more powerful.
  • 100% sustainable fuel.
  • Overtake is a separate electrical-power aid with an event-specific energy allowance defined by the FIA map.
  • Active aerodynamics let the cars switch between a high-downforce and a low-drag straight-line mode.

What to look for at this circuit

  • Deployment matters most where the full-throttle run is longest — watch the longest straight for where Overtake deployment is most valuable.
  • Energy-rich layouts let drivers deploy more freely; energy-limited ones force lifting and coasting.
  • Overtake detection and activation points are defined per circuit in the FIA document.
  • Practice and qualifying generally run a more permissive power curve than the race — expect Sunday straight-line speed to look weaker than Saturday's.

Updated Brazil power-unit elements

FIA Document 30 lists new components for five drivers for the remainder of the competition. Its Technical Delegate identifies Verstappen's fifth ICE, turbocharger and exhaust against four allowed; Leclerc and Hamilton's fifth ICEs against four; and Perez's fifth ICE and turbocharger plus fourth MGU-K against three. Each is stated as not compliant with the relevant allowance. The same report says Verstappen's sixth PU-ANC, Perez's fifth and Russell's sixth are within the six-part PU-ANC allowance. Component counts are not Stewards' penalty decisions or a qualifying classification. Formula1.com Stewards' Documents 35 and 37 impose 10-place drops for Leclerc (Car 16) and Hamilton (Car 44), respectively, for their fifth ICEs. The Stewards' later Document 33 imposes a 30-place drop on Verstappen for the fifth ICE, turbocharger and exhaust. Verstappen qualified 16th and the official Starting Grid placed him 21st. The Technical Delegate's Document 30 did not determine Perez's exact position; the later Stewards' Document 39 imposes a 30-place drop for Perez's fifth ICE, fifth turbocharger and fourth MGU-K. Perez qualified 21st and the official Starting Grid placed him 22nd. Document 30's fifth PU-ANC for Perez remains within that element's allowance. The later FIA Technical Delegate's Document 42 reports Hamilton's sixth ICE, fifth TC and EXH, fourth ES, PU-CE and MGU-K, and seventh PU-ANC. The source states each is beyond its season allowance. The Stewards' Document 43 imposes a further 65-place drop for these parts, in addition to Document 37's 10-place drop for the fifth ICE. Car 44 qualified 11th; the official Formula1.com Starting Grid places Hamilton 20th. The Race's earlier post-penalty order was provisional; use the official table for starting positions. This component-by-component FIA report supersedes the earlier team statement that Hamilton would start from the back without detailed counts. The separate Document 15 imposes a five-place drop for Russell's sixth ICE (sixth against four allowed). The Technical Delegate's 8 November Document 55 reports Arvid Lindblad's seventh ICE and sixth turbocharger (four allowed each), and Gabriel Bortoleto's sixth energy store and sixth PU-CE (three allowed each). It states all four elements are not compliant, although the replacements were approved by the Technical Delegate. It is not a Stewards' penalty decision and does not establish a grid penalty. FIA Technical Delegate Document 58 also lists the new Car 41 ICE and TC and Car 5 ES and PU-CE among approved parc-fermé replacements; it is a technical report, not a penalty decision. Separate Stewards' Documents 56 and 57 each impose a 10-place drop for this Race. FIA Document 59's final Starting Grid applies both penalties: Lindblad starts 18th and Bortoleto 19th. The Formula1.com snapshot retrieved at 16:09 UTC still showed Lindblad 12th. The official Starting Grid page checked at 18:40 UTC now agrees with Document 59, including Stroll 16th, Bottas 17th, Lindblad 18th and Bortoleto 19th. A results collector snapshot at 18:36 UTC briefly reverted Lindblad to 12th; keep Results bound to the hash-reviewed FIA grid so a transient cache response cannot replace it. FP1 and FP2 technical checks

FIA Technical Delegate Document 26 records checks before, during and after Friday's sessions, including fuel-energy flow, energy-store state of charge, ERS recharge limits, MGU-K limits, gear ratios and tyre monitoring. After FP2, car 55 was weighed and rear-brake pressure control was checked on cars 63, 12, 16, 44, 30 and 43. Fuel and engine-oil samples from cars 43 and 23 were analysed; the report says they were consistent with approved references and that all listed items complied with the 2026 Technical Regulations. FIA Document 26, pages 1–4.

FP3 and qualifying technical checks

FIA Technical Delegate Document 49 records checks during FP3 and Qualifying, including energy-store and ERS limits, MGU-K limits, engine and fuel systems, tyre pressures and use, and aerodynamic components on cars 30 and 87. Fuel and oil samples were analysed, and the report says all listed items complied with the 2026 Technical Regulations. It also lists the FIA Standard ECU software versions used by each team; no individual version is transcribed here. FIA Document 49, all five pages.

Race scrutineering — FIA Document 77

The Technical Delegate's report records race-weekend checks of the cars, power-unit systems, fuel and oil, followed by post-race weight and component inspections. Car 23 was selected for an additional throttle-pedal assembly inspection; the report states that all checked items and car weights complied with the 2026 Technical Regulations. This is a technical inspection report, not a Stewards' ruling. The five substantive pages are available in the FIA document gallery below. FIA Document 77.
