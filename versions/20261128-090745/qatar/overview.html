1. Session outcome2026-11-27

Antonelli leads extended FP2 as Gasly's spin brings a red flag

Kimi Antonelli set 1:23.077, 0.127 seconds ahead of George Russell; Lando Norris was third at +0.445s. The FIA Document 21 table matches Formula1.com across all 22 classified drivers. Two red flags interrupted the session, which was extended by 13 minutes. Gasly's high-speed spin brought out a red flag; he said cooler tyres and lower pressures led to more bottoming, and reported floor damage to be repaired for Saturday. A Virtual Safety Car followed when Hamilton stopped after reporting lost power. After Mercedes topped both Friday sessions, Norris estimated McLaren was 0.6–0.7 seconds per lap off and expected only a modest gain from its newer floor on Saturday. The Race's traffic-adjusted FP2 analysis put Antonelli's long-run average 0.810 seconds per lap ahead of Verstappen.

Why it matters: The official report does not establish a cause for Hamilton's loss of power. Gasly's explanation is his account, not a confirmed mechanical fault; Norris's gap is a Friday estimate, not a qualifying result. The Race's traffic-adjusted long-run figure is its own calculation from an interrupted session, not a representative race-pace forecast.

Sources: Formula1.com Practice 2 classification / Formula1.com Qatar FP2 report / The Race, Mercedes' Qatar pace analysis, 27 November / FIA Document 21, page 2 / Formula1.com Gasly on his FP2 spin / Formula1.com Norris on McLaren's Friday pace

3. Tyre wear and allocation2026-11-27

Localized tyre-wear indicators prompt a Pirelli review

The Race reports localized 100% wear indicators on the most-abused front-left tyres after 15 laps of high-fuel running. Pirelli is examining returned tyres and has not announced a pressure increase or shorter stint limit. FIA Document 13 V2 still specifies a 27-lap cap and 29.0 psi minimum front slick starting pressure; Document 25 reports listed checks compliant but publishes no revised table. Formula1.com still conflicts on pre-weekend sets (2/3/8 plus a Q3 Soft versus 2/4/7); neither is a remaining-set inventory.

Why it matters: A localized wear indicator is not evidence that a whole tyre wore through. Treat any pressure or stint-limit change as pending until Pirelli or the FIA confirms it, and keep the allocation discrepancy attributed.

Sources: The Race, 27 November tyre-wear report / Pirelli official Qatar tyre briefing, 24 November / FIA Document 13 V2, page 2 / FIA Document 25, page 4 / Current FIA Qatar event index / Formula1.com Qatar tyre preview, 10:05 UTC / Formula1.com 27-lap explainer, 10:43 UTC

4. Red Bull balance2026-11-27

Verstappen says Red Bull is still searching for balance

Verstappen finished fifth in FP2 after a day when he and Hadjar struggled with understeer. Verstappen described the balance as disconnected; technical director Pierre Wache said the fixes moved the car in the right direction but he was unsure how much of Mercedes' gap Red Bull could close.

Why it matters: This is a Friday assessment rather than a prediction of qualifying or race pace.

What the circuit demands

A fast, flowing, medium-to-high-speed loop originally built for motorcycles. Almost no slow corners and very high sustained lateral loads — physically brutal and historically the hardest race of the year on tyre construction.

Weekend format

Conventional weekend — three practice sessions, Qualifying and the Grand Prix.

  • All session times are shown in Lusail (AST) and Tallinn (EET).
  • Live weather is attached to each session on the Schedule & Weather page.
  • Session results appear on the Results page as soon as each session finishes.

Weekend storylines

  • The FIA's Qatar power-unit map is published and reviewed: use its 1.0-second Overtake detection gap, and keep electrical Overtake separate from the four Straight Mode zones.
  • Sergio Perez carries a five-place grid drop from Las Vegas; keep that separate from Qatar's own event sanctions.

Overtaking picture

Moderate. The main straight offers a genuine chance, but the flowing middle sector makes following closely difficult and rewards clean air.
