5. Las Vegas fallout2026-11-26

Verstappen/Russell tension and Ferrari clash follow F1 to Qatar

Formula1.com's 25 November Qatar preview recounts the continuing Verstappen-Russell verbal dispute and Ferrari's Las Vegas fallout: Charles Leclerc hit team-mate Lewis Hamilton during their Turn 7 fight. FIA Document 60 found Leclerc wholly responsible and imposed a 10-second penalty; team principal Fred Vasseur described the incident as unacceptable. At Thursday's FIA press conference, Leclerc said Ferrari needed a solution, accepted responsibility for the Vegas clash, and declined to disclose whether the rules of engagement would change. He also denied having a contractual number-one clause. Neither statement establishes the team's operational race instructions. A 26 November media-day report quotes Hamilton saying securing second in the Constructors' Championship is Ferrari's immediate priority, the drivers remain free to race, and process/communication improvements will be discussed over winter. The Race's Qatar day-one report says Antonelli described a Mercedes software safety feature added after the Vegas battery-deployment loss; this is a reported team change, not a change to FIA limits or a validated Qatar outcome. Formula1.com's Russell/Verstappen update adds that Russell found some post-race comments "quite amusing", is moving on and does not want to continue the off-track exchange; Verstappen said there was nothing else to add and that his focus was Qatar.

Why it matters: The Vegas penalty is final and does not become a Qatar ruling. Leclerc's contract denial and the drivers' comments do not establish team race instructions or a new rule. Attribute the Mercedes software update to The Race; do not treat it as an FIA energy-limit change or a confirmed on-track result. Keep Russell and Verstappen quotes attributed, not as predictions.

What the circuit demands

A fast, flowing, medium-to-high-speed loop originally built for motorcycles. Almost no slow corners and very high sustained lateral loads — physically brutal and historically the hardest race of the year on tyre construction.

Weekend format

Conventional weekend — three practice sessions, Qualifying and the Grand Prix.

Weekend storylines

  • The FIA's Qatar power-unit map is published and reviewed: use its 1.0-second Overtake detection gap, and keep electrical Overtake separate from the four Straight Mode zones.
  • Sergio Perez carries a five-place grid drop from Las Vegas; keep that separate from Qatar's own event sanctions.

Overtaking picture

Moderate. The main straight offers a genuine chance, but the flowing middle sector makes following closely difficult and rewards clean air.
