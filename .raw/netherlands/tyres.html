Tyres & Strategy

Pirelli's official compound choice and tyre prescriptions for Zandvoort, from the FIA event documents.

FIA-confirmed: compounds for this event are the C2 (hard), C3 (medium) and C4 (soft) — the same trio used at Zandvoort last year. Mandatory race tyres (Pirelli's Doc 2): C2 and C3.

Compound allocation

Pirelli's weekend preview

  • “The track, situated just 40 km from Amsterdam, offers a low level of grip both because of the nature of its asphalt… and because of the sand blown onto the circuit from the nearby beaches,” Pirelli's preview reads — grip can shift from one session to the next.
  • “The circuit is particularly demanding on tyres in terms of energy density, owing to its short lap length and numerous corners… the two banked turns… generate extremely high vertical and lateral forces on the tyres.”
  • Aero-downforce demand is high — “very similar to… Budapest.”
  • Sprint format means only one hour of practice to settle on a set-up before parc fermé.
  • “The hard is likely to be the reference compound for Sunday’s race, [while] the two softer options could both prove effective choices for Saturday’s Sprint.”

Pressures & camber (FIA Doc 2)

  • Slicks — front min. 26.0 psi / expected ≥27.0 psi, camber limit −2.75°; rear min. 25.0 psi / expected ≥26.0 psi, camber limit −1.5°.
  • Intermediates — front min. 27.5 psi / ≥28.5 psi, camber −3°; rear min. 26.5 psi / ≥27.5 psi, camber −2°.
  • Wets — front min. 26.0 psi / ≥28.5 psi, camber −3°; rear min. 25.0 psi / ≥27.5 psi, camber −2°.
  • Maximum tyre-blanket heating time is 2 hours for all three tyre types.
  • Cut-off for fitting tyres ahead of Sunday's race: 4 hours before the start.

What this circuit does to a tyre

High-energy, continuous loading with very little straight-line rest, so front-left and rear temperatures build. Graining on the softer compounds is the usual complaint; track evolution across the weekend is strong.

Why strategy matters here

Hard. Effectively one real passing place — into Turn 1 out of the banked final corner, with DRS. Track position and the undercut usually decide it, which is why strategy and safety-car timing carry so much weight here.

With passing so hard-won, the undercut and safety-car timing usually decide the race far more than raw pace.
